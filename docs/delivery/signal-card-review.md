## Findings

- **Permit records materially outnumber project identities.** The Zilker display card correctly groups 19 Austin permits for the same Barton Springs address and the Oct 2–4 / Oct 9–11 event. Four sampled Ascension Seton permits share 1201 W 38th St and master permit `13449254`; they describe phases of one sterile-processing renovation, not four independent projects. The PACU/Surgery permits at 919 E 32nd St also share master permit `13732494`. Preserve the permit rows as evidence, but do not treat card or permit counts as a distinct-project count.
- **“Active” is not a current-demand date.** The Austin row for permit `2023-106541 BP` remains Active despite its 2023-08-21 issue date. Other Austin rows show 2025 issue dates and recent `statusdate` refreshes; a status refresh is not evidence of a newly started project. Chicago stage permits remain ACTIVE after their scheduled event end dates because their permit expiration dates are later. Reassess against the described event/work date.
- **Event signals have narrow, explicit windows.** The Zilker event is upcoming in October. Chicago’s Clark St stage permit ends Sep 27 (the review date); Sista Strut setup is Oct 2/event Oct 3; the Bears stage is Oct 4. None of these selected events had a scheduled end date before the Sep 27 review date: Clark St is on its final scheduled day and the others are upcoming. Recheck/age out promptly after each source-described event window; the sample therefore does not claim a fully expired event. Permitted event stages do not by themselves prove uncovered temporary-generation demand.
- **Initial Zilker batch-query failure was not treated as evidence.** The first attempt incorrectly sent 19 permit numbers in a single equality filter and got an empty array. That raw response is retained for audit only. A corrected exact `IN` query returned all 19 permit rows; the table links the corrected query and the manifest labels the failed attempt separately.
- **One Georgia filing contains direct bridge-generation evidence.** EPD’s Azalea Quarry narrative says four Tier 4 diesel generator sets will power the quarry for the first two years after startup or until grid power is available, whichever occurs first. This supports an explicit temporary-generation bridge. It still does not prove that the sets are rented, unawarded or available for outside supply; verify procurement, MW, start and grid date before any tier change.
- **Other generator permits describe standby assets.** TADG ATL02, QTS Fayetteville and Apex Site Solutions filings describe emergency/backup generator fleets. Those facts do not prove portable-rental demand, but the dated equipment activity remains a discovery Signal until current stage and power scope are resolved. QTS ATL3 is a stronger construction Signal because its EPD narrative describes a proposed greenfield data center; campus/backup-generator facts must not be repurposed as construction MW.
- **Chicago’s temporary-service permit is a useful discovery fact, not a rental proof.** Permit `3459792` specifies two 2,000A temporary electrical services and identifies Northwestern Memorial Hospital, Gurtz Electric and Power Construction. Amps alone do not establish MW, duration, a generator requirement or an uncovered procurement package.
- **Several cards have weaker or unresolved commercial fit.** Like-kind asphalt equipment replacement, onboard-diesel quarry equipment, routine generator replacements, a lab tenant-improvement permit, and an early mine EIS notice still qualify as exploratory Signals while their dated activities are relevant. Investigate current scope and buyer; close only after evidence confirms completion, no additional relevant activity, or adequate supply. Keep reported permit/capacity facts separate from the hypothesis that a customer will rent temporary power.

No records were promoted and no database or product code was changed. Decision labels below are source-quality findings, not tier changes. Unknown MW alone is not a rejection reason. A weak commercial hypothesis keeps its Signal value when the dated source event plausibly supports discovery; rental need remains an inference until confirmed.
