# Fetch the engineering documents, not the listings

GridLens's equipment-demand model has no validated records because of two gaps, and neither one is about coverage. The pipeline fetches index pages and summaries instead of the engineering PDFs those pages link to, and the parser cannot read the tables where ratings are printed. The fastest route to documented equipment records is a deterministic, coordinate-preserving table extractor, followed by fetching the documents GridLens already knows about. Those documents are TCEQ NSR application PDFs (`{project}-ac.pdf` / `-tc.pdf`), Oklahoma DEQ evaluation memoranda (`storedpermits/{N}.pdf`), New Mexico NMED application PDFs with their standard Table 2-A, and FERC Resource Reports for the pipeline dockets already found through the Federal Register. The documents we inspected show a structural problem every source shares. **Air-permit equipment tables list emission units, so electric-motor drives (which emit nothing) usually appear only in narrative text or process flow sheets.** That holds in a verified New Mexico gas-plant application and in an Oklahoma Title V memo. Under GridLens's rule that "no electric drive evidence means no electrical MW," the parser has to read narrative sections as well as tables. FERC Resource Reports, which state the driver type explicitly, will yield proportionally more electrical records than state air permits. For the other sectors, the best new feeds are cheap structured discovery and phase signals rather than equipment documents:

- **Data centers:** FAA obstruction filings for cranes and stacks, the Texas Comptroller data-center registry, and Oklahoma DEQ's data-center permit table.
- **Mining:** MSHA's weekly mine file and TCEQ's aggregate-operations dataset.
- **EV charging:** the AFDC station dataset, whose API host has moved.

Equipment-grade evidence for those sectors is still sparse, and most of it sits in NEPA documents, SEC filings, and air permits for on-site generation. The extraction pipeline can run with **$0 in API fees**. Digital PDFs are parsed locally on CPU, scanned pages are OCR'd locally or deferred, and deterministic rules replace the LLM proposal step. The cost is worker CPU time and lower recall on scans, not correctness. The largest unresearched area is Texas TCEQ beyond the NSR pending listing (Title V, PBR registrations, Records Online, TERP award detail). TCEQ also moved new NSR application documents to a JavaScript portal on Aug 31, 2026, so the cheapest Texas win may already be partly closed for new filings.

## The bottleneck is the parser and the fetch target, not the source list

The coordinator's review of the gl3 repo identifies the root cause. Equipment-demand-v1 is deployed. Its parser (`ingestion/parsers/equipment.py`) accepts only labeled, line-oriented text, and the 39 enabled registry entries deliver listings, summaries, permits-as-rows, and news. The TCEQ NSR pending adapter already parses links to per-project application PDFs at `tceq.texas.gov/assets/public/permitting/air/reports/applications/{project}-ac.pdf` and `-tc.pdf`. It downloads only the short plain-language summary PDFs, and only for filings with data-center or power names (coordinator review of gl3). Adding sources will not help until the system can turn a ruled equipment table into span-verified claims. The verified documents show why. Oklahoma memoranda put engines in Emission Unit Group tables, with rows such as "2,370-hp Caterpillar G-3608 LE 4-stroke, lean-burn with oxidation catalyst" ([OK DEQ storedpermits/8014.pdf](https://applications.deq.ok.gov/permitspublic/storedpermits/8014.pdf)). New Mexico's Table 2-A interleaves its cells when text is extracted naively, and its Existing/New checkbox state can't be recovered from the text layer ([AQBP-Application-6567M6](https://www.env.nm.gov/air-quality/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2020/08/AQBP-Application-6567M6.pdf)). The Texas Enterprise Fund award PDF also scrambles its columns when converted to plain text ([TEF Award Listing Data](https://gov.texas.gov/uploads/files/business/TEF_Award_Listing_Data.pdf)).

## Oil and gas: driver type decides whether a record counts

### Air permits document gas engines well and electric motors poorly

Oklahoma's `storedpermits/{N}.pdf` packages are the cleanest state documents inspected. They contain an evaluation memorandum, draft Specific Conditions, and Standard Conditions in one text-layer PDF. Their EUG tables carry unit ID, hp, make/model, serial number, and install date. The verified ONEOK Ward/Northern Rows station lists **five 1,380-hp Caterpillar G-3516B and eight 2,370-hp G-3608 LE engines, all gas-fired** ([OK DEQ 8014](https://applications.deq.ok.gov/permitspublic/storedpermits/8014.pdf)). The same PDF lists WC-5 as 1,380 hp in the memo table and 1,340 hp in the permit conditions. A single document can contradict itself, so the claim store must keep both values with their own spans rather than pick one. N appears to be a sequential key shared with `comment.aspx?key={N}`, and the same scheme survived a domain migration from `deq.state.ok.us` ([legacy 2309.pdf](https://www.deq.state.ok.us/apps/nondiv/permitspublic/storedpermits/2309.pdf)). That makes polite integer enumeration feasible. It is untested, though, and rate limits and robots.txt were never observed because the research proxy blocked direct probes. For issued permits, DEQ points requesters to Central Records rather than to a searchable archive ([DEQ data-center page](https://oklahoma.gov/deq/deq-information-on-data-centers.html)), so `storedpermits` covers drafts noticed for comment, not every issued permit. The West Edmond electric-motor finding (Siemens 3 × 2,500 hp in `8406.pdf`) that motivated this research **could not be re-fetched and remains unverified**.

New Mexico is the more ingestible state structurally. A public ArcGIS MapServer exposes seven permit-class layers as JSON/GeoJSON with a 2,000-record page size ([mercator air_facilities](https://mercator.env.nm.gov/server/rest/services/aqb/air_facilities/MapServer?f=pjson)). Application PDFs sit at date-pathed WordPress upload URLs, and the standard Table 2-A carries unit number, description, rated capacity, make/model, dates, RICE ignition type, and serial number. The verified Black River Gas Processing Plant application shows the central problem: rows ENG-1 to ENG-4 are 2,250-hp Waukesha gas engines, but **"Electric Inlet Compression" appears only in the Section 4 process flow sheet**, not in Table 2-A or the exempt-equipment list ([AQBP-Application-6567M6](https://www.env.nm.gov/air-quality/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2020/08/AQBP-Application-6567M6.pdf)). The current GCP-O&G form has no electric-driver inventory at all ([GCP-O&G form 2025](https://www.env.nm.gov/air-quality/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2025/06/AQBP-GCP-OG-Form-2025.04.29-1.pdf)). APMAP excludes NOI, No-Permit-Required, and exemption facilities, and newly permitted facilities' documents take about a month to upload ([APMAP guide](https://www.env.nm.gov//air-quality/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2021/09/AQBP-APMAP-Instructions-19.07.23.pdf)). NM OCD's imaging server and OGRID/`fCS` facility IDs are entity-resolution assets rather than equipment sources. For example, Thoreau Compressor Station #5 resolves to Transwestern, OGRID 329750, fCS00000000030 ([OCD filing](https://ocdimage.emnrd.nm.gov/imaging/Filestore/SantaFe/NF/20260413/nAUTOfCS000709_04_13_2026_02_41_43.pdf)).

These permits still matter when they show only gas engines. A documented gas-fired driver is affirmative evidence that the compression load is *not* grid electric. Record it as a documented non-electric driver, not as zero MW and not as "unknown." Gas units that generate power at a site belong in the supply ledger, separate from demand.

### FERC Resource Reports carry the explicit electric-drive evidence

FERC filings state the driver because driver choice determines air impacts. The verified CP24-8-000 order for NGPL's Texas-Louisiana Expansion adds **"a new 18,340 HP electric motor driven (EMD) compressor unit"** at Compressor Station 302 in Texas, bringing certificated capacity to 48,340 hp across four EMD units ([FERC Order CP24-8](https://aglaw.psu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/NGPL-FERC-Authorization-12.09.24.pdf)). At 0.7457 kW/hp, the new unit is about 13.7 MW of motor nameplate, a "calculated" amount class. Federal Register notices state hp more consistently than driver type. CP26-24 states both ([FR 2025-21709](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-12-01/pdf/2025-21709.pdf)). CP26-478 gives "one compressor unit of 11,110 horsepower" with no driver ([FR 2026-09561](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-05-13/pdf/2026-09561.pdf)). The automation path is to parse hp and driver from FR notices GridLens already ingests, mark the driver unknown when it is absent, and fetch the application or Resource Report 1 from eLibrary for TX/OK/NM dockets.

eLibrary has no documented API or RSS. Supported channels are the Docket Sheet, the Daily Filings search, and eSubscription email, which needs a free FERC Online account and delivers links "typically… within several hours of filing" ([eLibrary Quick Help](https://ferc.gov/elibrary-quick-help); [eSubscribe](https://www.ferc.gov/how-to-esubscribe)). A dedicated inbox parsed by code is the only sanctioned push channel. CEII and privileged volumes are off-limits. One coverage caveat is unverified: major Permian takeaway pipelines are believed to be intrastate or Hinshaw systems, so their compressors would appear only in TCEQ permits. That makes the incomplete TCEQ research more costly.

EPA sources play a supporting role. ICIS-Air has no unit, capacity, or horsepower fields ([ICIS-Air download summary](https://echo.epa.gov/tools/data-downloads/icis-air-download-summary)). The 2023 NEI public point data come as process-level SCC summaries, and unit-level reports are restricted ([EPA NEI 2023](https://gaftp.epa.gov/air/nei/test/where-can-i-obtain-the-2023-nei-data.html)). NEI can only ever show gas-fired units, which makes it negative evidence. GHGRP is stalled: the latest public summaries cover RY2023, RY2025 reporting moved to Oct 30, 2026, and EPA proposed ending 46 of 47 source categories ([GHGRP deadline fact sheet](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2026-02/fact_sheet_ghgrp_reporting_deadline_february2026.pdf)). Don't build dependencies on Subpart W.

## Data centers: each lifecycle phase has a different public footprint

No single source follows a campus from land deal to expansion, but each phase leaves a characteristic public trace. The table below maps them. Bold entries are sources GridLens does not ingest today.

| Phase | Best public signals | What it yields for demand |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-development | **OK HB 2992 60-day land-purchase notices** to OCC/county; **TX Comptroller Qualifying DC / Large DC Project registry**; JETI; Ch. 312 abatements; NM county IRBs; NM OSE water actions | Existence, owner/operator keys, timing. No MW. |
| Construction | **FAA OE/AAA** cranes and structures; TDLR TABS cost/dates; TCEQ stormwater NOIs (now distorted by the pause) | Start of vertical construction. No public source for temp-service kW. |
| Commissioning | Generator air authorizations (OK DEQ sequential permits, TCEQ certifications); ERCOT approved-to-energize | A window, not a value. Load-bank load is mostly genset-served. |
| Operations / ramp | ERCOT approved vs observed aggregate; SEC filings; OCC/NM PRC contract dockets | Site MW for public registrants, ramp calibration |
| Expansion | New air-permit modifications; Comptroller "Large Project" list; FAA filings near existing campuses | Incremental phase timing |
| On-site supply | OK DEQ data-center permit table (Major permits = turbines/large genset fleets); NMED fuel-cell permit; TCEQ turbine applications | Supply ledger, kept separate from demand |

The Comptroller registry lists about 67 qualifying data centers and 111 large data-center projects. Its registration numbers distinguish owner, occupant, and operator (`DC######-OW1/OC1/OP1`, `LD######`). It has no county, MW, or download link, so it has to be scraped from HTML ([Comptroller DC lists](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/data-centers/data-center-lists.php)). Oklahoma's HB 2992, effective Jul 1, 2026, requires notice to the OCC, county commissioners, and abutting landowners **within 60 days of a land purchase** ([KGOU](https://www.kgou.org/energy/2026-05-13/stitt-signs-bill-to-prevent-higher-utility-costs-from-data-centers-into-law)). That could be the earliest statutory signal in the three states, but nobody has confirmed whether the OCC publishes the notices.

The FAA OE/AAA web services are the best construction signal found. They are RESTful, return XML, and need no key or login. They return lat/lon, AGL height, sponsor, and a structure type such as `CRANE$MOBILE`, with an 8,000-row query cap ([FAA OE/AAA guide](https://oeaaa.faa.gov/oeaaa/downloads/external/content/External%20Web%20Services%20Guide%20V_2022-NOV.pdf)). Sponsors are often crane contractors rather than owners, so matching has to be spatial: join filings to known campus parcels within about 2 km.

Commissioning and ramp have one excellent aggregate anchor. ERCOT reported **9,456 MW approved to energize versus 4,132 MW observed** non-simultaneous peak for September 2026 ([ERCOT Sept 2026 overview](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/10/16/ERCOT-Monthly-Operational-Overview-September.pdf)). That works out to about 44% of approved large load actually showing up. Use it as a ramp prior, not as a site-level value.

The Texas permitting pause is now the dominant interpretive hazard. On Sept 21, 2026, TCEQ was directed to issue no permits for data-center projects until the ERCOT and TWDB audits finish ([Troutman](https://www.troutman.com/insights/governor-abbott-directs-tceq-to-halt-all-data-center-permits-pending-ercot-twdb-audits/)). TCEQ says applications can be submitted but "will not advance." Records as of Oct 16 nonetheless show 7 air certifications and 11 stormwater approvals after the pause, and the ERCOT audit is expected to finish in late November ([Community Impact](https://communityimpact.com/bastrop-cedar-creek/texas-legislature/texas-continued-processing-some-data-center-permits-after-gov-abbott-s-pause-records-show/)). From Sept 21 until the pause lifts, a missing Texas permit says nothing about a project. GridLens should tag affected projects "pending — paused" rather than "stalled" and expect a burst of issuances when the pause ends.

In Oklahoma, the DEQ data-center page (modified Oct 28, 2026) is a ready-made, county-level index of air and water permits with links to PDFs. It covers Google's Pryor, Stillwater, and Muskogee entities, Pioneer Trail, Acacia ("OSG" minor permit), Core Scientific MSK1/2, and others ([OK DEQ data centers](https://oklahoma.gov/deq/deq-information-on-data-centers.html)). Add it immediately. Its "Major" permits for data-center LLCs almost certainly mean large on-site combustion, which goes in the supply ledger. In New Mexico, Project Jupiter's NMED fuel-cell air permit is stayed in the state Supreme Court, and the related OSE water use is paused ([Source NM](https://sourcenm.com/?p=31964)).

Commercial databases such as Baxtel are quote-priced CSV snapshots with undisclosed license terms ([Baxtel datasets](https://baxtel.com/services/datasets)). They belong in internal QA of the campus inventory, not in product data.

## EV, mining and C&I: abundant discovery data, scarce equipment evidence

### EV charging relies on program rules more than documents

Charging sites rarely file equipment documents publicly, so nameplate usually comes from program rules. NEVI requires **≥4 ports capable of simultaneous ≥150 kW** ([ODOT RFP 2026](https://oklahoma.gov/odot/about-us/newsroom/2026/odot-seeks-proposals-to-implement-additional-electric-vehicle-ch.html)). TxDOT's Phase II awarded $226M for **321 stations** on Oct 15, 2026 ([TxDOT](https://www.txdot.gov/about/newsroom/stories/txdot-expands-electric-vehicle-charging-network.html)), which implies at least about 190 MW of rule-derived nameplate across those sites. That is an "inferred" figure and a ceiling, because power-sharing cabinets cut real demand.

Tesla's map shows 19 planned Semi sites in Texas at up to 1.2 MW per dispenser ([electrive](https://www.electrive.com/2026/02/27/tesla-identifies-66-sites-for-proprietary-truck-charging-network/)). The NM CFI truck centers at Lordsburg and Vado have 9 pull-through stalls each, with no kW stated ([EnergyTech](https://energytech.com/emobility/article/21280735/terawatt-infrastructure-and-new-mexico-dot-receive-63m-grant-for-heavy-duty-charging-corridor)).

The AFDC dataset is still updated daily and includes planned and private stations. Its publisher is now the "National Laboratory of the Rockies," with docs at developer.nlr.gov, so **any hard-coded developer.nrel.gov URL is an ingestion-breaking change to check** ([data.gov AFDC](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/alternative-fueling-stations)). FY25 FTA Low-No awards in TX/OK/NM contain no battery-electric bus or charging projects, so transit-depot evidence has to come from earlier rounds ([FTA FY25 awards](https://www.transit.dot.gov/funding/grants/fy25-fta-bus-and-low-and-no-emission-grant-awards)). One widely circulated Houston METRO depot profile is internally inconsistent and should not be used without METRO primary sources ([mgrid.org](https://mgrid.org/?p=5007)).

### Mining needs MSHA for activity, permits for equipment, and SEC filings for crypto MW

MSHA's Mines file refreshes **every Friday**. It carries MINE_ID, status and status date, operator and controller IDs, SIC, lat/lon, `PORTABLE_OPERATION`, and the shift fields ([MSHA OGI](https://arlweb.msha.gov/OpenGovernmentData/OGIMSHA.asp); [definition file](https://arlweb.msha.gov/OpenGovernmentData/DataSets/Mines_Definition_File.txt)). Diffing weekly snapshots finds new and reactivated sites. Days × shifts × hours gives an operating-hours proxy that turns connected kW into MWh once an equipment record exists. The portable flag separates likely genset-powered crushers from grid load.

The TCEQ Aggregate Production Operations dataset (`9ixa-7k3m`, updated Oct 26, 2026) is the Texas quarry and frac-sand discovery feed, available as CSV or GeoJSON ([data.gov APO](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/texas-commission-on-environmental-quality-aggregate-production-operations)). The sibling `9kvs-ig69` dataset is stale. New Mexico MMD's app exports every registration to Excel ([EMNRD MMD apps](https://www.emnrd.nm.gov/mmd/mmd-online-web-applications)). Oklahoma's Department of Mines requires no equipment list in non-coal applications ([ODM guidelines](https://oklahoma.gov/content/dam/ok/en/mines/documents/non-coal-minerals-forms-publications/permit/Guidelines_and_Summary_052026.pdf)).

Equipment and energy detail for mines lives in NEPA documents for federal-land projects, such as the live 2026 Santa Rita (Chino) MPO amendment, whose stated purposes include "utilities" ([Grant County Beat](https://grantcountybeat.com/nm-news/non-local-news-releases/blm-seeks-input-on-proposed-copper-mine-plan-of-operations-amendment-near-silver-city)), and in rock-crusher and engine air permits, which were not researched. BLM's NEPA Register is a Power Apps portal with GUID project URLs and no documented API ([BLM ePlanning](https://eplanning.blm.gov/?f=pjson)). Use Federal Register BLM notices as the machine-readable trigger and fetch documents selectively.

For digital-asset mining, the Texas VCM rule requires facilities over 75 MW to register ESIID, load zone, five-year peak-load forecasts, and interruptible share ([PUCT VCM](https://www.puc.texas.gov/industry/electric/business/vcm)). Nobody has found evidence that the registry is public, which is the most valuable open question in this sector. Until it is answered, SEC disclosures are the documented source. Riot reported **700 MW approved at Rockdale with a 351 MW 2025 average load, and 1 GW at Corsicana with a 335 MW average** ([Riot Q4 2025 call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/03/02/riot-riot-q4-2025-earnings-call-transcript)). The gap between approved capacity and average load is exactly the distinction GridLens has to preserve between interconnection capacity and realized demand.

### C&I sources are discovery feeds; equipment lives in permits and specs

Texas has the best structured incentive data. The TEF cumulative PDF ("as of September 30, 2026") needs table-aware parsing ([TEF listing](https://gov.texas.gov/uploads/files/business/TEF_Award_Listing_Data.pdf)), and TSIF awards appear as press releases with a stable slug. The Comptroller's Ch. 312 and SB 1340 agreement databases are high-value discovery sources GridLens lacks, though their export behavior is untested ([Comptroller Ch. 311/312](https://comptroller.texas.gov/economy/development/search-tools/ch311-312-reports.php)). Oklahoma's Quality Jobs open dataset was last updated in 2019 ([data.gov](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/oklahoma-quality-jobs-incentive-payments)).

TWDB's CWSRF Project Information Form collects average and peak flow but **no hp, kW or kWh fields** ([CWSRF PIF SFY27](https://www.twdb.texas.gov/financial/programs/CWSRF/doc/SFY2027/CWSRF-PIF-SFY27.pdf)). Any wastewater-plant load estimate from it is "inferred." Texas emergency engines under PBR 106.511 require **no registration** ([TCEQ PBR 106.511](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/air/permitbyrule/subchapter-w/portable_power.html)), so no complete public backup-genset registry exists in Texas.

Among licensed sources, only Dodge's Project Documents endpoint (plans and specs, including Division 26 electrical) offers a path to equipment schedules ([Dodge APIs](https://www.construction.com/apis/)). Neither Dodge nor IIR advertises MW fields, and redistribution terms are unknown.

## Extract tables from the text layer, verify every value by span

Use the "structure from a model, text from the PDF" pattern. Docling's TableFormer predicts the table structure and matches it back to PDF text cells, "avoiding re-transcription." It processes typical tables in **2–6 seconds on CPU**, and pages at about 0.6–1.7 s without OCR ([Docling technical report](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.09869v5)). Camelot's `ml` flavor likewise "fills cell text from the PDF's text layer… so the model can't alter values" ([Camelot comparison](https://camelot-py.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user/comparison.html)). Generative VLM parsers lead OmniDocBench. PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 scores 94.76 table TEDS ([OmniDocBench](https://github.com/OpenDataLab/OmniDocBench)). But they rewrite text and are GPU-oriented, so their output could count as "documented" only after re-verification against a text layer. PyMuPDF's AGPL license is a consideration for a commercial SaaS.

The recommended pipeline has six stages:

1. **Snapshot and route pages.** Hash each document (SHA-256). For each page, record extracted character count and image coverage. Image-dominated pages with little text go to a `pending_ocr` state rather than a paid service (see the zero-fee policy below). Record `text_source` on every claim as native, embedded OCR, or GridLens OCR with the engine and version.
2. **Build a canonical text layer.** Map every character offset to its word and bbox.
3. **Run deterministic table extraction first.** Use a ruled-grid pass with pdfplumber, PyMuPDF, or Camelot lattice, then Docling or Camelot hybrid/ml as the fallback for borderless or multi-line tables. Write custom stitching for multi-page tables using repeated headers and column x-alignment.
4. **Normalize headers and units deterministically.** A synonym table maps headers (e.g., "EU / Unit ID / Source ID" → `unit_id`) and stores the header cell's bbox as provenance. A unit lexicon includes OCR confusions such as "1,5OO". kVA→kW requires an assumed power factor and is therefore "inferred". MMBtu/hr is fuel input and never converts to electric kW without a documented efficiency.
5. **Use deterministic rules for the residue, not a paid LLM.** Narrative sections, where electric drives hide, are scanned with versioned phrase patterns ("electric motor-driven", "electric drive", "Electric Inlet Compression", "VFD", "motor hp" near a compressor term) and hp/count regexes bound to the same sentence or paragraph. Tables that fail to parse stay "extraction incomplete". An optional later step can add a small *locally hosted* model that proposes values with a verbatim `evidence_quote` and page number. It goes through the same verifier, with no external API.
6. **Verify every value.** Accept a value only when its quote matches the page text exactly after recorded normalization and the value string appears inside the quote's span. Fuzzy matches go to an internal review queue, never to auto-acceptance. Store the char span plus the union bbox. The LLM's self-reported confidence is ignored.

LangExtract (Apache-2.0) implements this aligner pattern. It labels matches exact, lesser, or fuzzy and drops ungrounded output ([Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/introducing-langextract-a-gemini-powered-information-extraction-library/)). The four-phase algorithm is small enough to reimplement ([Elixir port docs](https://lang-extract.hexdocs.pm/LangExtract.Alignment.Aligner.html)). Claude's PDF citations return `cited_text` and a page range but **no bbox**. They don't work on scanned PDFs, and they can't be combined with structured outputs ([Claude citations docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/build-with-claude/citations)). They are useful as a propose-and-quote layer, but GridLens must still locate the quote in its own text layer to get coordinates.

### Zero-fee extraction policy

GridLens should pay **no per-page or per-token API fees** for extraction. Every stage runs on the existing worker:

| Stage | Free tool (local, CPU) | Cost driver | Provenance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Digital-PDF tables | pdfplumber / Camelot lattice first; Docling TableFormer fallback | ~0.6–1.7 s/page + 2–6 s/table → roughly 2–10 CPU-hours per 10,000 pages | Native text bbox |
| Narrative driver evidence | Versioned regex/phrase rules over the canonical text layer | Negligible | Native char span + bbox |
| Scanned pages, phase 1 | **Deferred** as `pending_ocr`; counted, not guessed | None | n/a (unknown, not zero) |
| Scanned pages, phase 2 | Tesseract or PaddleOCR (CPU) on the deferred queue, rate-limited to idle worker time | Slow (EasyOCR >30 s/page; Tesseract is typically faster, unmeasured here) | OCR word geometry, labeled `gridlens_ocr` with engine/version |
| Residual proposals (optional, later) | Small locally hosted model, same quote-and-verify contract | Local CPU/GPU time only | Verified span only |

The trade-offs are throughput and recall, not correctness. Scanned and oddly laid-out documents yield fewer records, and OCR'd values carry a lower-confidence extraction method, so they never pass as native text-layer evidence. Every accepted value is still verified against source text. Measure the real scanned-page share in phase 1 before investing in OCR. The previously considered paid options (AWS Textract tables at ~$150 per 10,000 pages, Mistral OCR 4 at ~$20–40) stay out of the pipeline.

The gold set is internal evaluation infrastructure. It should cover 50–100 permits stratified by agency, digital versus scanned, and ruled versus borderless versus multi-page tables. Score field-level precision, "provenance precision" (stored bbox IoU > 0.5 against gold), and abstention rate, with precision as the gate. The research notes suggested reusing the labeling page as a production review UI. Product canon forbids routine human workflows in the user-facing product, so it must stay internal.

## Snapshot, diff and key everything by the regulator's own identifiers

Every fetch should store raw bytes, SHA-256, URL, fetch time, and HTTP validators. Claims then point at an immutable document version rather than a mutable URL. Several sources are bulk snapshots whose value is the diff: MSHA Mines.zip (weekly), TCEQ APO (CSV), the Comptroller DC lists, the TEF PDF (quarterly "as of" date), the OK DEQ data-center table, and AFDC (daily). Diff on the native primary key and emit status-transition events, such as a new MINE_ID or an Active-to-NonProducing change. Don't re-ingest whole files as new evidence. For query APIs with caps, page by date windows: FAA's 8,000-row limit and Regulations.gov's roughly 5,000 records per query both need re-queries on `lastModifiedDate` ([Regulations.gov API](https://open.gsa.gov/api/regulationsgov/)).

Amendment lineage should hang on permit numbers, not document keys. Oklahoma's storedpermits integer is a per-notice document key. The permit number (e.g., `2020-0510-TVR` with suffixes such as -TVR3, -M, -O, -AD) carries lineage. New Mexico encodes revision directly (6567M6 is the sixth modification; P198R4 is the fourth Title V revision). FERC uses docket and sub-docket plus accession `YYYYMMDD-XXXX` ([eLibrary help](https://ferc.gov/elibrary-quick-help)). A later revision supersedes an earlier one for "current" state, but both remain evidence. Intra-document conflicts, such as the 1,380 vs 1,340 hp in OK 8014, should be stored as two claims and flagged, never averaged.

Entity resolution should rely on the regulators' own keys and use names only as a fallback:

- **Facility:** OK AQD Facility ID, NM AI number, NM OCD OGRID plus `fCS…` facility ID, ICIS-Air PGM_SYS_ID.
- **Mines:** MSHA MINE_ID with operator and controller IDs.
- **Data centers and projects:** Comptroller DC/LD registration numbers, JETI IDs.
- **Construction:** FAA ASN, joined spatially.
- **Schools:** NCES ID for Clean School Bus districts.
- **Unverified keys:** SEC CIK, EIA plant ID, and TCEQ RN/CN still need confirmation.

Status vocabularies need GridLens-native mappings with provenance, especially "pending — paused" for Texas data-center filings and "approved but not observed" for ERCOT large load.

Treat unofficial endpoints conservatively. There are no published rate limits for eLibrary, OK DEQ, or NMED. That argues for ≤1 request/second, off-hours backfills, and declared user agents (inferred, not agency-stated). Third-party scrapers listed on parse.bot are not a substitute for official channels.

## Conclusion

The research changes the picture of where electrical MW evidence comes from. State air permits, the documents most often assumed to be equipment-rich, are built around emissions. They document gas engines precisely and electric motors only in passing. GridLens's first validated records will therefore come mostly from well-documented *non-electric* drivers and on-site supply, with electrical demand records arriving through FERC Resource Reports, narrative sections, and a minority of state permits. That supports the canon of unknown-is-never-zero and supply-separate-from-demand rather than straining it. It also means success metrics for equipment-demand-v1 should count documented driver determinations, not just MW records.

The second implication is temporal. Texas data-center permits are frozen, and TCEQ has moved new filings behind a JavaScript portal, so the cheapest Texas document channel and the meaning of "no permit" are both in flux this quarter. Building the extraction core and the Oklahoma, New Mexico, and FERC document fetchers now protects GridLens against that volatility. Finishing the TCEQ research before the pause lifts means the expected burst of Texas issuances lands in a pipeline that can actually read it.
