# Texas power sources — October 23, 2026

Checked against public endpoints on October 23, 2026. A 200 response was not treated as a parse success. Registry rows are the enabled definitions. This note does not claim a scheduled production run.

The ranking followed the v2 behind-the-meter, bridge, and temporary-power source notes. A generator, turbine, or filing is tagged `btm`, `bridge`, or `temporary` only when its own text uses that language. Air-permit operating hours are classified by `config/sources/air-permit-hours.v1.json`: 500 hours per year or less is backup, 2,000 through 6,000 hours per year is bridge/temporary, and 8,760 hours per year with "onsite use only" in the same clause is behind-the-meter. The clause is the sentence. A semicolon does not split it, and neither does an abbreviation period such as "U.S."

A hyphen, en dash, em dash, or the word "to" between two hour figures is one range. Both endpoints are kept, in the order written, and a decimal stays on the endpoint it follows. The range is not replaced by its right-hand number, so "12-500 hours per year" is the range 12 through 500 and is not read as 500. The same annual phrasing used for a single figure applies to the range ("per year", "annually", and the `hrs`, `hr/yr`, and `h/yr` forms). "up to 500 hours per year" stays a single cap of 500. A range is classified only when both endpoints fall in the same band, including the same-clause onsite test for an exact 8,760. "12-500 hours per year" is backup because both ends are at or under 500. "12-2,000 hours per year" crosses the backup band and the bridge band, so it stays unclassified. "2,000-6,000 hours per year" is bridge/temporary. A range with an endpoint in no band stays unclassified.

Portable or trailer-mounted wording is bridge/temporary only when that same clause names power generation: a generator, genset, gen-set, generator set, turbine, or electric or power generation. The bare word generator counts. A portable generator, portable generator engines, an expiring or temporary permit for generators, a propane generator, and a gas-fired generator are that context. The exclusion list removes reviewed non-electric compounds — hazardous waste generator, steam generator, waste generator, small quantity generator, gas generator, and ozone, nitrogen, oxygen, hydrogen, vapor, signal, smoke, and aerosol generator — even when a fuel or duty word is stacked in front. Process-gas "gas generator" is on that list. A gas-fired generator is not. The word engine is not that context by itself, so an engine-driven crusher or pump is not tagged from "engine". An expiring or temporary permit uses the same test. A rock crusher or other non-power plant that is merely called portable, or that holds a temporary permit, is not bridge/temporary.

A negation governs the noun phrase it attaches to, not the rest of the sentence. The cue (no, not, without, never) counts when it sits before that phrase, after the last comma, semicolon, or and/or/but before the phrase, and no more than six words away. "No." before a number is a label. A comma or semicolon stops the cue from reaching the next phrase. It does not split the sentence used for hours and "onsite use only". An affirmative generator in the same sentence still tags, so "no turbines; portable diesel generators" and "portable diesel generators, not turbines" are bridge/temporary. Negation does not change an hours band. Backup, bridge, and behind-the-meter still follow the figure, so 8,760 hours per year with "onsite use only" stays behind-the-meter when the sentence also says the units do not generate electricity for the grid, or that no power generation is exported. "No power generation", "not used for power generation", "does not generate electricity", and "without generators" still block a portable or temporary tag when that phrase is the one negated. The next sentence can still tag. Each air-permit record stores the rule id and the matched evidence. Conflicting clauses stay unclassified.

Only New Source Review plain-language-summary rows carry summary text into that hours rule. Construction NOI rows and electric-generating-unit search rows do not fetch a summary. They classify the fields on the row itself, so an hours limit that exists only in a summary PDF is not visible on those two sources.
