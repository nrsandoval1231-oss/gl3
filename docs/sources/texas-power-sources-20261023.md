# Texas power sources — October 23, 2026

Checked against public endpoints on October 23, 2026. A 200 response was not treated as a parse success. Registry rows are the enabled definitions. This note does not claim a scheduled production run.

The ranking followed the v2 behind-the-meter, bridge, and temporary-power source notes. A generator, turbine, or filing is tagged `btm`, `bridge`, or `temporary` only when its own text uses that language. Air-permit operating hours are classified by `config/sources/air-permit-hours.v1.json`: 500 hours per year or less is backup, 2,000 through 6,000 hours per year is bridge/temporary, and 8,760 hours per year with "onsite use only" in the same clause is behind-the-meter. The clause is the sentence. A semicolon does not split it, and neither does an abbreviation period such as "U.S."

A hyphen, en dash, em dash, or the word "to" between two hour figures is one range. Both endpoints are kept, in the order written, and a decimal stays on the endpoint it follows. The range is not replaced by its right-hand number, so "12-500 hours per year" is the range 12 through 500 and is not read as 500. The same annual phrasing used for a single figure applies to the range ("per year", "annually", and the `hrs`, `hr/yr`, and `h/yr` forms). "up to 500 hours per year" stays a single cap of 500. A range is classified only when both endpoints fall in the same band, including the same-clause onsite test for an exact 8,760. "12-500 hours per year" is backup because both ends are at or under 500. "12-2,000 hours per year" crosses the backup band and the bridge band, so it stays unclassified. "2,000-6,000 hours per year" is bridge/temporary. A range with an endpoint in no band stays unclassified.

Portable or trailer-mounted wording is bridge/temporary only when that same clause names electric power generation. The bare word generator is not that context. A generator counts when an electric, power, diesel, natural gas, emergency, or standby modifier sits immediately before it. "Fired" may follow a fuel modifier, those modifiers may stack, and the word engine may sit between them, as in "diesel engine generator" or "natural gas-fired generator". A genset, gen-set, generator set, turbine, or the phrase electric generation or power generation also counts. Natural gas counts. Bare "gas" does not, so a process-gas generator or a gas-fired generator is not power context. The allowlist is the gate: a denylist would miss the next unlisted non-electric use. The exclusion pattern is a second gate for the reviewed compounds — hazardous waste generator, steam generator, waste generator, small quantity generator, gas generator, and ozone, nitrogen, oxygen, hydrogen, vapor, signal, smoke, and aerosol generator — so those phrases cannot count even with a fuel or duty word stacked in front. The word engine is not that context by itself, so an engine-driven crusher or pump is not tagged from "engine". An expiring or temporary permit uses the same test. A rock crusher or other non-power plant that is merely called portable, or that holds a temporary permit, is not bridge/temporary.

A negated power statement suppresses bridge, backup, and behind-the-meter for that whole sentence. "No power generation", "not used for power generation", "does not generate electricity", and "without generators" are that kind of statement. The cue governs the power phrase when it sits before the phrase, after the last and/or/but in the sentence, and no more than six words away. "No." before a number is a label, not a negation. And, or, and but do not start a new clause; the sentence does. The next sentence can still tag. An hours figure in a sentence that does not negate power generation keeps its band. "Does not generate electricity" is recognized as a negation. Affirmative "generate electricity", without the allowlist above, is not power context. Each air-permit record stores the rule id and the matched evidence. Conflicting clauses stay unclassified.

Only New Source Review plain-language-summary rows carry summary text into that hours rule. Construction NOI rows and electric-generating-unit search rows do not fetch a summary. They classify the fields on the row itself, so an hours limit that exists only in a summary PDF is not visible on those two sources.
