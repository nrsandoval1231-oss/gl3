# Temporary and rental power screening

Official permit rows are screened for dated, site-bound **Signals** that may justify a human follow-up about rental or bridge power. The permit description and issue date are reported evidence. The rental question is an inference. A permit never proves that a rental generator is required, that utility service is unavailable, or that an outage will occur.

The current rule categories are:

| Activity | Signal activity | What the permit supports | What remains unknown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Existing generator, ATS, switchgear, or transformer replacement/changeout | Backup power | Equipment change at a named site; continuity power may be worth asking about | Interruption, rental requirement, load, buyer, supply, start, and duration |
| Explicit temporary, rental, mobile, portable, or construction power | Temporary power | Temporary power activity is stated | Whether rental supply is still needed, load, buyer, supply, start, and duration |
| Utility/service/energization delay explicitly tied to bridge power | Bridge power | The reported delay and bridge-power relationship | Whether a rental is required, load, buyer, supply, start, and duration |
| Dated event with electrical, stage, sound, concession, or powered production activity | Event power | A powered event activity is described | Rental requirement, load, buyer, supply, start, and duration |
| Supported major facility construction or remodel | Facility expansion | The existing facility activity rule | Activity-specific load, buyer, supply, start, and duration |
| Hospital surgery/PACU or similar interruption-sensitive remodel | Backup power | An activity where continuity may merit review | No outage or interruption is asserted; other power facts remain unknown |

Rows must have an eligible current status (Active, Issued, or Approved), a source citation, an issue date, and a site. Final, void, withdrawn, completed, expired, or canceled work is rejected. Explicitly provided, secured, operational, or load-supplying **temporary or rental** generation closes that same activity. A bare required-power phrase, a fulfilled requirement, or a negated/no-longer-needed requirement does not reopen it. The screen evaluates sentence, semicolon, and newline clauses independently: an explicitly additional, separate, or unserved temporary activity remains a Signal, and its retained clause identifies the remaining activity in the rationale. A permanent backup generator at a site does not cover an explicitly distinct construction-power need. Installation without operating, supplied, or load-serving evidence does not establish adequate temporary supply. Incidental mentions in signs, catalogs, roof/grease/racking/lighting maintenance, office paint, generic event placards or seating, and permanent generation without load-side activity do not qualify. Ordinary facility opportunities continue through the facility activity rules.

For powered event rows, full-year dates explicitly present in the work description determine whether the event is still active; permit issue and expiry dates do not substitute. The end date remains eligible through that calendar day and is re-evaluated starting the following day. If event timing is not explicitly parseable, the rule does not infer it from the permit date; the row remains unaccepted for the event rule and timing is unresolved.

Every accepted row remains a Signal. No permit date is treated as the work start, no area or cost becomes MW, proposed generation is not suitable supply, and unknown utility availability is not a shortage. The interface and stored assessment retain the reported description separately from the inferred rental hypothesis.

Permit identity remains row-level. Multiple permits for one site or event can describe related phases or vendors; the system does not merge them or report them as unique projects. Counts in the retained-row preview are permit records, not independent opportunities. Source coverage in this slice is limited to Austin, Seattle, and Chicago retained snapshots.
