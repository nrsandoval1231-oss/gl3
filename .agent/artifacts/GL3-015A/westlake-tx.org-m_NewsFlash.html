  • Introducing The Broadmoor House

    What started as a handful of friends following Stephanie Trantham's home decorating journey has grown into a community of more than two million people looking to create designer-inspired homes on a budget. Her lifestyle brand, The Broadmoor House, focuses on achieving a high-end look through affordable décor and do-it-yourself projects. Now a business owner in Westlake, Trantham has found new opportunities to create trending content while balancing life as a working mom.

    When Stephanie Trantham and her husband began building their first home in Haslet, the term "home influencer" was virtually unheard of in the area. As she connected with other women who were also building homes, she started a group chat where they could share ideas and discuss all things home design. Eventually, they encouraged her to create a social media page documenting the construction and decoration of their home on Broadmoor Drive.

    Becoming an influencer was never part of Stephanie's original plan; she was simply sharing projects she loved with friends. In fact, she considered herself an introvert. But when the COVID-19 pandemic hit and her husband lost 40% of his salary, Stephanie decided to postpone deleting the account. "My account really grew during COVID because everybody was focusing on being at home," Trantham said. That's when her page really began to take off.

    Stephanie's relatable approach resonated with followers looking to renovate their own homes. Without any formal education in interior design or woodworking other than her experience as a former junior high art teacher, her early videos focused on teaching simple DIY projects, such as replacing cabinet hardware or swapping out light fixtures, allowing homeowners to tackle updates without hiring a handyman.

    Although the house on Broadmoor Drive was complete, The Broadmoor House was just getting started. Stephanie and her husband, Ben, opened an office at the Terraces at Solana, where their team of videographers, photographers, marketers, and other creative professionals helps meet the growing demand from affiliate partners while producing high-quality content.

    As her audience grew, so did the business behind the brand. Today, The Broadmoor House not only produces social media content for its own channels but also creates marketing content for brands in the home and lifestyle industries. Rather than relying on traditional advertising,

    Stephanie focuses on authentic product recommendations and real-life demonstrations. As Stephanie's audience grew, so did her content, expanding beyond home improvement to include beauty, fashion, and everyday lifestyle topics.

    Despite the demands of running a business built around social media, Stephanie says her family always comes first. "That's something that I learned two and a half years ago. Luckily, it was pretty early on in my career," Trantham said. "As much as I say that I work a lot, we work hard, but we play hard. We try to create really special memories for our kids. I try to put down my phone and give it a minute."

    Working in Westlake gives Stephanie the flexibility to step away from content creation when her family needs her, allowing her to build both a successful business and meaningful memories with her children.

    Named after the street where it all began, The Broadmoor House has grown from a passion project into a thriving lifestyle brand. Today, Stephanie inspires millions of homeowners with affordable design ideas, DIY projects, and practical tips that show creating a beautiful home doesn't have to come with a luxury price tag.

  • History of Mahotea Boone

    Long before roads were paved and homes were built, the Mahotea Boone Trail was believed to be a campground and burial site for early Native Americans. According to the historical marker for the Star Stagecoach Route just east of the area, travelers had several encounters with Native Americans living in what are now Tarrant and Denton counties, making this claim a possibility.

    Surrounded by the oak trees and prairies of the Cross Timbers, the land was developed into a residential subdivision in the early 1970s by Bill Boone. Mahotea Boone has fourteen lots, eleven of which currently have older homes. It is zoned for a minimum of two-acre lots and appears to be redeveloping with larger homes. Boone chose the name Mahotea, pronounced (Ma-ho-ta), after his wife’s grandmother’s childhood friend from the Chickasaw Nation. Bill himself was related to the famous American frontiersman and pioneer of the late 1700s, Daniel Boone.

    One of Westlake’s oldest subdivisions, Mahotea Boone Trail was designated a Texas Historical Marker in 2017. The marker was unveiled by the Westlake Historical Society during a dedication ceremony at the intersection near J.T. Ottinger Road, bringing Westlake’s neighbors together to gain a shared knowledge of our history.

    Mahotea Boone, one of the many reasons Westlake is Distinctive by Design!

  • The Truth About Westlake's Data Center

    Westlake has always been intentional about responsible growth. Every decision made by Town leaders is guided by one simple question: Does this make Westlake better for the residents and businesses who call this community home? The new data center coming to Westlake is no exception.

    From the very beginning, Town leaders understood that a project like this would naturally bring questions and concerns. They also understood that simply ignoring potential challenges was not an option.

    This project is not about choosing growth over quality of life. It is about creating a stronger, more financially sustainable future while protecting everything that makes Westlake special.

    Explaining the Location

    One of the most important factors in any development decision is location. The data center will be located off Highway 377, just south of the Town’s existing pump station, in an established commercial and industrial area. We know there has been opposition to this project from many Keller residents whose neighborhood borders this Westlake property. It’s important to understand the timeline associated with this land and why it was selected for this project.

    This parcel of land has been zoned for commercial and industrial use since 1998. The land that is now the Marshall Ridge neighborhood in Keller was also zoned for commercial use until Keller’s city council voted to change the zoning classification to residential in 2006 through their Ordinance 1309.

    Then in 2015, Keller’s city council approved Ordinance 1742, which changed the zoning from industrial commercial to residential, which added 15 more homes to Marshall Ridge, this time directly along the fence line of the Westlake property that remained zoned for commercial and industrial. You can watch that meeting here.

    At no point did council or residents raise concerns about building homes that close to the property in Westlake that was zoned for commercial and industrial uses. In fact, many residents who spoke at the meeting were in favor of building the homes, citing a desire to avoid commercial development in that piece of land. Commercial property would have created a buffer between the neighborhood and what would eventually be built on the land in Westlake.

    While Keller’s zoning for their property changed, Westlake’s vision of the adjacent land within our Town limits has not. An industrial or commercial project like a data center, a large distribution center, or something similar was always expected, hence why our zoning hasn’t changed in 28 years.

    The question has been asked: Why not just move the project farther North on Highway 377? The answer is simple. Texas is a strong property rights state.  The owner of this property does not own another site in Westlake, and the location allows for access to the highway and the electrical infrastructure needed for the project. 

    Bottom line: Westlake believes that a modern data center, with the right regulations in place, would be a better neighbor to residential development than the other industrial, distribution, warehousing, and manufacturing uses allowed by right on this property. The land has been zoned for commercial and industrial use for the last four decades; the landowner wants to build a data center on their property and has the legal right to do so; and since this is the only property the owner owns in Westlake, the project cannot be moved to another location. While many would have preferred the land to remain a pasture, that was not a realistic long-term expectation for property that has been zoned for commercial and industrial use since 1998.

    Behind the Decision

    On Tuesday, August 4th, Westlake’s Planning & Zoning (P&Z) Commission approved the site plan for this project with three conditions as part of the development review process. It’s important to note that P&Z did not have the authority to vote on the use of the land. They only had the authority to vote on the site plan’s conformance with PD, Planned Development District 3-8 (PD-3-8) standards.

    Under PD-3-8, data centers are already a permitted use on that property. Because that land use has previously been established, the commission's responsibility was limited to determining whether the site plan complied with the Town's adopted development standards and the approval criteria outlined in Ordinance 307.

    The same remains true for Town Council. They will not vote on whether a data center should be on that land; that’s already been established. They will only vote on a development agreement.

    The purpose of the development agreement is to ensure the proper installation and care of infrastructure serving the project and to include other requirements as necessary to improve architecture and landscaping. The development agreement is legally binding, and it runs with the land. Again, it does not speak to the land use, as this has already been established and is allowable by right. This means that the Town Council does not have the authority to deny the use once the application has been accepted.

    Further, P&Z did ask for additional improvements from the developer. They requested an 8-foot berm or sound barrier wall along the southern property line; additional architectural sound barriers or acoustic louvers around mechanical equipment such as HVAC units, cooling towers, and generators; and more evergreen and hardwood trees to create even greater visual screening from neighboring properties.   These items have been added to the developer’s agreement.

    Westlake will continually hold the development to the Town’s codes and ordinances and report any violations outside of our purview to the TCEQ or other appropriate state or federal authorities.

    A Thoughtful Decision for Generations of Westlake Residents

    The new data center is not simply a new building coming to Town. It represents careful planning, responsible leadership, and an investment in Westlake’s future.

    Town leaders did not overlook concerns. They did not assume problems would resolve themselves. They identified potential challenges and addressed them before construction begins.

    They protected water resources. They strengthened the power infrastructure. They required noise and aesthetic protection. And they created a financial opportunity that reduces reliance on residential taxpayers and did so in an area of town that has been zoned for commercial and industrial use since 1998. This is what responsible growth looks like.

  • Planning & Zoning Recap: Data Center

    During a public meeting on Tuesday, the Westlake Planning & Zoning Commission approved the site plan with conditions for a data center campus on approximately 87.9 acres along Highway 377. The meeting included a presentation from the developer, questions from commissioners, and several hours of public comment from neighboring property owners.

    The developer presented plans for a four-building data center campus that includes a private electrical substation and backup generators. You can see their presentation here. Commissioners reviewed every aspect of the proposed site plan, including the building architecture, landscaping, drainage, traffic access, utilities, setbacks from neighboring properties, and environmental considerations.

    The proposed development exceeds many of the standards already required by the property's Planned Development zoning. Among the features included in the plan are nearly 400 new trees and approximately 4,000 native trees and shrubs, a 75-foot landscaped buffer, berms ranging from 8 to 15 feet in height, and building setbacks that are significantly greater than the minimum requirements. The closest home will be 506 feet from the building line; that’s well above the 150 feet that are required.

    Throughout the evening, commissioners listened carefully to residents who shared concerns about potential noise, visual impacts, and how the project could affect nearby neighborhoods. Those comments led to an extensive discussion about whether additional screening and sound mitigation measures could be added to the project beyond what the Town's zoning regulations already require.

    As a result of those discussions, the Planning & Zoning Commission requested several additional improvements. The developer agreed to consider incorporating these enhancements, including an 8-foot berm or sound barrier wall along the southern property line, additional architectural sound barriers or acoustic louvers around mechanical equipment such as HVAC units, cooling towers, and generators, and more evergreen and hardwood trees to create even greater visual screening for neighboring properties.

    An important part of the discussion centered on the commission's legal role in reviewing the application. Town staff explained that this meeting was a site plan review, not a hearing on whether a data center should be allowed on the property. Under the property's existing Planned Development zoning (PD-3-8), data centers are already permitted on that land. Because that land use has previously been established, the commission's responsibility was limited to determining whether the site plan complied with the Town's adopted development standards and the approval criteria outlined in Ordinance 307.

    After reviewing the application, Town staff concluded that the site plan satisfied every applicable approval requirement and, in several areas, including architecture, setbacks, buffering, and landscaping, exceeded the Town's minimum standards.

    Given the site plan’s compliance with the applicable approval criteria, there was no available basis on which the Commission could deny the site plan. The Commission did so while also adding the three additional conditions intended to further strengthen the project's visual screening and sound mitigation for neighboring properties.

    The meeting concluded after several hours of discussion and public feedback. To reiterate, the Planning & Zoning Commission did not have the authority to vote on the use of the land, just the site plan, which met all the requirements of PD-3-8.

    The project will now continue through the Town's established review process, with future consideration by the Westlake Town Council as required.

    We know you may still have several questions about the project. We have created an extensive FAQ webpage that will answer many of those questions. We’ve also created an easy-to-understand white paper that explains each aspect of the project, including water, electricity, noise, and traffic. The economic impact of this project is also outlined.

    We would like to thank those who attended and spoke during Tuesday night’s meeting and encourage everyone to visit the links in this recap to gain a better understanding of this new project.

  • Westlake Data Center Response

    Town of Westlake Statement Regarding the Planned Data Center

    The Town of Westlake would like to provide additional context regarding the planned data center that will be located off U.S. Highway 377 in Westlake. We understand that some concerns have been raised by residents in the City of Keller due to the project’s proximity to the Keller city limits, and we believe it is important to provide the public with the complete timeline and facts.

    Contrary to recent assertions that this project came as a surprise, the proposed data center was discussed openly and collaboratively more than a year ago. In December 2024, leaders from the Town of Westlake, the City of Keller, and Hillwood Properties met specifically to discuss this proposed development. Keller was represented at that meeting by its former mayor, who served until May 2026, as well as the current Keller City Manager.

    During that meeting, the proposed development was presented, including a preliminary site plan. The location, size of the facility, anticipated noise levels, water usage, electrical infrastructure, and other project details were reviewed. Westlake officials emphasized that protecting neighboring residents would be a priority throughout the planning process.

    Specifically, Westlake committed to working with the developer to:

    • Position the facility as far north on the property as possible, exceeding the planned development’s required setback.
    • Ensure the facility complies with Westlake’s noise ordinance, which is more stringent than those of many neighboring communities.
    • Require extensive landscape screening, including mature vegetation, to help visually buffer the site and further reduce sound.

    At the conclusion of that meeting, the project was met with support from Keller’s leadership team, which expressed the belief that the development would have minimal impact on Keller residents. Based on those discussions, Westlake had no reason to believe there were outstanding concerns during the following year and a half.

    More recently, Keller’s current mayor, who served as Mayor Pro Tem with Keller at the time of the meeting, and some current City Council members have stated they were unaware of the project. Why the information from the December 2024 meeting was not communicated to subsequent elected officials, the full City Council, or Keller residents is not something the Town of Westlake can speak to. We will say that Westlake’s entire Town Council was aware of this project at the time, further highlighting our emphasis on transparency. The project has also been discussed publicly through Westlake’s Planning and Zoning Commission meetings and at the Town Council meeting in January 2025. The meetings were publicly noticed, live-streamed and archived, identified the project as a data center, and provided opportunities for public comment. No opposition was raised during those proceedings. Any suggestion that this project was developed in secret or without the knowledge of Keller officials is simply not accurate.

    Since the project’s inception, Town staff has worked closely with the developer to minimize impacts on neighboring properties. The facility will meet all applicable safety standards and comply with Westlake’s noise ordinance.

    In addition, several enhancements have been incorporated into the project beyond ordinance requirements, including:

    • A landscaped berm along U.S. Highway 377.
    • A minimum 75-foot landscape buffer along the southern property line.
    • Mature vegetation planted throughout the berm and surrounding landscape to further screen the facility and enhance sound attenuation.
    • An increased building setback beyond what is required under the planned development standards to further improve buffering.

    The closest Keller residence will be approximately 506 feet from the building line, with the enhanced landscaping, berm, and increased setbacks providing additional separation. The closest Westlake residence is approximately 1,200 yards away, or 2/3 of a mile.

    Westlake and Keller leaders will meet soon to further discuss this project. “The Town of Westlake values its long-standing partnership with the City of Keller. Our communities have enjoyed an excellent working relationship for many years, including Westlake’s contract for police services with the Keller Police Department. We appreciate that partnership and remain committed to maintaining a positive and collaborative relationship moving forward”, says Westlake Mayor Kim Greaves.

    Our goal is not to create division, but to ensure the public has accurate information about this project and the extensive planning that has occurred. Westlake has approached this development thoughtfully and transparently, with careful consideration given to protecting neighboring residents while supporting responsible economic development. We remain committed to continuing to answer questions, listen to concerns, and work cooperatively with our regional partners throughout the process.

  • Westlake Firefighters' Elite Training

    Before a firefighter enters a burning building or a paramedic provides lifesaving care, countless hours are spent preparing for those moments. At Westlake Fire-EMS, maintaining that readiness is a year-round commitment.

    The Department is focused on training, fitness, and professional and personal growth. “Our priority is helping personnel address issues early so they can maintain long-term health and continue serving safely,” Fire Chief John Ard said.

    To best serve the community, firefighters and paramedics are held to high standards of medical and physical readiness. As part of this commitment, personnel receive annual physical exams that meet the National Fire Protection Association’s (NFPA) 1580 standard. They have also far exceeded annual fitness requirements needed for the Department of Public Safety’s Row Test and the Department’s Physical Ability Test Course.

    In March, Westlake Fire-EMS held its annual physicals in partnership with Front Line Mobile Health. Front Line provides specialized medical services to public safety organizations nationwide. Their physicians and staff include former firefighters, military officers, combat medics, and other public safety professionals who understand the unique demands of the job.

    During this year’s evaluations, Front Line conducted a variety of routine exams, including chest x-rays, vision and hearing tests, cancer screenings, and exercise assessments. Due to the intense nature of emergencies in the field, Firefighters and paramedics face increased risks of heart disease, exposure to carcinogens, and mental health challenges.

    If a concern is identified during a physical, the department works closely with the employee and medical professionals to ensure follow-up care, wellness resources, or temporary duty modifications are provided as needed.

    While annual physicals are required for the safety and health of staff, Department leadership is committed to helping each firefighter and paramedic maintain a long and fulfilling way of life. “Early detection and prevention help ensure our personnel remain healthy, fit for duty, and capable of delivering superior service to the community,” Chief Ard said.

  • Westlake's Historic Cemetery

    Underneath still, quiet trees lies one of our community’s greatest pieces of history. Westlake Cemetery holds generations of stories carved into weathered tombstones. Long before our “Westlake Together” campaign, many of those buried here worked to make their community a better place through selfless acts of kindness and public service.

    Located off Highway 170 at 3101 J.T. Ottinger Road, this serene spot was formerly known as the Roanoke Independent Order of Odd Fellows (IOOF) Cemetery. The fraternity’s mission was to “engage in humanitarian efforts such as visiting the sick, relieving the distressed, burying the dead, and educating orphans.” The cemetery was not limited to members or Westlake residents, however, with burials including railroad workers and World War I and II veterans dating back to the 1800s.

    While strolling among the unique headstones decorated with flowers and flags left by visitors, one can find people like Claude Fanning. Born in Roanoke in 1893, Fanning served in the Medical Detachment of the Field Artillery during World War I. His son, Charles Fanning, later served as president of the Roanoke IOOF Cemetery Association, which partnered with Westlake to coordinate preservation efforts.

    The cemetery was maintained by the Roanoke IOOF Cemetery Association for decades. But as the trees aged and the surrounding area developed, the Association entrusted the Town of Westlake with the care and restoration of this final resting place for generations of community members. By 2010, the name had officially changed to Westlake Cemetery. Today, the Town partners with Rodriguez Graveside Services to maintain the grounds and oversee in-ground burials and funeral services.

    Westlake Cemetery preserves stories that otherwise may have been forgotten. It is a beautiful place to sit, reflect, and remember the community that came before us.

  • April 2nd: Statewide Emergency Alert Testing

    On Thursday, April 2, 2026, at the request of Texas Division of Emergency Management (TDEM), a statewide test of emergency alerting systems will occur between 10:00 AM and 12:30 PM. This will only be a test. No emergency action is required.

    Residents may receive a test alert on their mobile phone and/or hear outdoor warning sirens during this time. Because jurisdictions across the region may participate in this test, you may receive more than one test alert on your phone during the testing window. 

    Testing helps ensure emergency alert systems function properly and allows agencies to evaluate how alerts are delivered during real emergencies.

    What are emergency alerts for?

    Emergency alerts are used to quickly notify residents about serious safety situations, such as:

    •  Severe weather
    •  Hazardous material incidents
    •  Evacuations
    •  Public safety emergencies

    Many people are familiar with AMBER Alerts, but emergency alerts can also be sent for other urgent situations that may affect community safety.

    What will the alert look like?

    Depending on your jurisdiction, your alert may look something like this:

    Denton County: TEST of emergency alert system. No emergency. No action needed.

    Denton County: PRUEBA. Prueba del sistema de alerta. No hay emergencia. No accion necesaria.

  • Town Hall/Court Closing on Fridays

    In our continued effort to serve you efficiently and responsibly, the Town of Westlake will transition to a 4-day workweek for administrative offices, effective the week of August 25. Town Hall will be open to the public Monday through Thursday, 8:00 am to 5:00 pm, and closed on Fridays. So, what does this mean to you?

    No Disruption to Essential Services The Fire and Police Departments will continue to operate 7 days a week. Our Public Works Department’s schedule will remain Monday through Friday without change, ensuring the safety and day-to-day needs of our community are always met.

    Saving Taxpayer Dollars This change is expected to save the Town an estimated $25,000 annually in utility costs by reducing energy usage and operating expenses on Fridays.

    Minimal In-Person Visits on Fridays Town Hall averages fewer than three walk-in visitors each Friday, with most guests arriving for pre-scheduled meetings. Shifting to a four-day schedule allows us to align our hours with when we are most needed, without sacrificing service.

    No Impact to Westlake Academy Services Shared services provided to the Academy will continue five days a week, and additional support will be provided upon request, ensuring no disruption to the school or its operations.

    Expanded Court Office HoursOur Municipal Court staff will now be available Monday through Thursday from 8:00 am to 5:00 pm. On court days, the court office will close at 3:30 PM to allow for proceedings scheduled to begin at 3:45 PM. Court staff will be available to assist the public following the adjournment of court.

    Improved Service and Employee Performance Studies show four-day workweeks help improve work-life balance, increase employee morale, reduce sick leave, and enhance overall customer service and productivity.

    Recruitment & Retention Tool Offering a modern, flexible schedule also positions Westlake to attract and retain the best talent, the very people who serve you every day.

    Access Services Anytime, AnywhereMany of our services, including permit requests, payments, service requests, and more, are available online 24/7 at westlaketx.gov, so there’s often no need to visit Town Hall in person.

    We believe this thoughtful shift reflects our commitment to innovation, efficiency, and top-tier service, and we’re proud to take this step in a way that benefits both our residents and our employees.

    If you have any questions about the new schedule or how to access services, we’re here to help. Just give us a call or visit us online.

  • Westlake's Budget Process

    At the Town of Westlake, financial stability isn’t just a goal, it’s a principle that guides every decision we make. As we develop the fiscal year 2025-2026 budget, we remain committed to long-term planning, conservative forecasting, and responsible stewardship of your tax dollars. This is the first of several communications we’ll be sharing to help keep you informed throughout the entire process.

    Goals for this budget include: 

    • Advancing our Town toward best practices

    • Preparing for future development

    • Ensuring our budget surplus for Capital Improvement Plan (CIP), equipment replacement, and lessening the debt need

    • Cutting departmental expenditures by at least 3%

    • Adjusting the benefit package to industry standards

    • Discussing CIP and Service Level Adjustments (SLAs), fund balance, and tax rate

    Here’s how our approach is delivering value today and preparing us for a successful tomorrow.

    A Strategic Approach to Financial Stability

    Westlake’s budgeting philosophy is built on forward-thinking and conservative planning. This strategy gives us the flexibility to adapt while safeguarding essential services, even in uncertain times. We take a measured approach to both revenue and expenditures:

    • On the revenue side, we forecast conservatively, basing estimates on historical trends and known factors, not optimistic assumptions.

    • On the expenditure side, we plan for the high end, knowing that costs will likely rise due to inflation, new contracts, or expanded services.

    We also maintain strong reserves. We are required to have an annual fund balance of 180 days in our general fund expenditures. This gives us a vital cushion to continue operations in the event of a disruption and positions us to act on unforeseen opportunities or emergencies.

    Before finalizing both expenditures and revenues, we are seeing a 2% decrease in expenditures compared to last year, while our revenues remain flat. This is after funding capital items and SLAs.   

    What We’ve Done So Far

    Our fiscal year 2025-2026 budget process began with the development of a CIP, a five-year roadmap outlining large-scale infrastructure needs, including roads, buildings, water, and sewer systems. Staff worked closely with Council to set minimum fund balances in most funds. Each department evaluated its assets and service goals to determine what’s needed to maintain quality and support future growth.

    With those major projects identified, the staff has:

    • Reviewed and adjusted departmental operating budgets

    • Submitted SLAs for any new or expanded programs

    • Aligned Westlake Academy’s budget by moving shared costs directly under the school’s oversight for greater transparency and control

    We also acted quickly in response to potential legislative changes that could have limited our ability to issue debt. Because our CIP was already in place, we proactively issued $9.5 million in bonds to fund top-priority projects such as:

    • Expansion of Westlake Academy’s Arts & Sciences building (with help from a $3 million donation)

    • Water infrastructure repairs and upgrades

    • Land acquisition and design work for a second water tower to serve future development on the west side of Town

    These projects ensure Westlake’s infrastructure is equipped to handle our continued growth for many years to come.

    How This Benefits Westlake Residents

    Our approach directly benefits residents by ensuring:

    • Service continuity and reliability, even in a downturn

    • Infrastructure that keeps pace with growth, without sudden tax spikes

    • Transparency and accountability in how public dollars are managed

    • Protection of Westlake’s character and autonomy in the face of state-level challenges to local control

    By preparing in advance, we’ve positioned ourselves to fund essential improvements without compromising day-to-day operations or raising unnecessary debt burdens.

    What’s Next: From Planning to Adoption

    We’re currently projecting $33 million in total revenue for fiscal year 2025-2026, including sales and property taxes, fees, interest earnings, and transfers. This funding will cover operations, capital projects, service level adjustments, and debt obligations for the upcoming fiscal year.

    Here’s what’s ahead:

    • July: We receive the certified property tax roll, which is the official valuation of all real property in Westlake

    • August: We will calculate and present the required property tax rates, including:

      • The No-New-Revenue Rate

      • The Voter-Approved Rate

      • The proposed rate for Council’s consideration

      • The De Minimus Rate

      • Council votes on the maximum proposed rate for the public hearing, public notice

    • September: Budget and Tax Rate Public Hearings and budget, revenue ratification, and tax rate votes

    At that point, we’ll share a detailed breakdown, including examples, comparisons to other cities, and charts, so you can clearly see what’s being proposed and how it impacts you. 

    Westlake’s financial strategy is proactive, transparent, and aligned with our community’s values. We’ve already:

    • Prioritized long-term capital needs

    • Balanced day-to-day operations

    • Taken decisive steps to protect our future infrastructure

    • And remained alert to legislative changes that could impact our autonomy

    As we head into the next stages of the budget process, our goal is to keep you engaged and informed. Together, we’ll continue building a community that’s strong, sustainable, and future-ready. This is what makes Westlake distinctive by design.

  • Lead and Copper Testing

    Dear Westlake Residents,

    We want to keep you informed about some recent water quality testing results within the Town of Westlake. As part of our ongoing commitment to your safety, we are conducting regular water quality testing, including tests for lead and copper levels. Recently, two samples from homes in Westlake showed elevated levels of lead, which is something we take very seriously.

    Our goal is to ensure your water is safe and to stay ahead of any potential issues. In fact, the Town has been working closely with the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) as part of a special initiative to investigate and remove any potential lead sources from the water system, and based on those efforts, we’ve confirmed that there are no lead service lines in the Town.  After receiving the test results, the Town of Westlake field verified that neither the service line to the residential water meter or the service line from the meter to the residence contained lead.  This indicates that the source of the lead presence found in the testing is most likely from the plumbing inside the home.

    For homes built after 1986, which include most homes in Westlake, it is highly unlikely that there is any lead plumbing, as federal laws were updated then to ensure that plumbing materials used in drinking water systems are lead-free. For older homes, while lead levels may sometimes show up in testing, it is often due to older plumbing fixtures or pipes inside the home, rather than the Town’s water supply.

    We are fully committed to monitoring the situation and have planned additional testing in 2025 to ensure the water remains safe. If your home was one of those affected, we will be in touch with you directly to keep you updated.

    Please know that your safety and well-being are our top priority, and we’re on top of this. If you have any questions or concerns, feel free to reach out to us. We are here to help. 

    If you’d like information on how to test water on your own, please contact the Town of Westlake Public Works at (817) 490-5732. Thank you for your understanding and for being a part of our community.

  • Westlake Academy: A Unique Partnership

    Westlake Academy: A Pillar of Excellence Elevating the Town of Westlake

    Westlake, Texas, has long been recognized for its natural beauty, low tax rates, and small-town charm. Yet, at the heart of what makes Westlake a true destination is Westlake Academy, an internationally recognized school that stands as a beacon of academic excellence. As one of the few International Baccalaureate (IB) public charter schools in Texas, Westlake Academy not only enhances the Town’s prestige but also plays a vital role in shaping its growth and appeal.

    The Academy was founded back in 2003 as an alternative to the three school districts that serve the Town of Westlake: Carroll, Keller, and Northwest ISDs.  Instead of our students being split between three districts and creating a divide in our community, the Academy became a place to bring our families together and provide an IB curriculum that wasn’t offered by the school districts. It was envisioned as a Westlake-based school to truly call our own.  Now, 80% of school-age Westlake kids currently attend Westlake Academy. 

    The Academy’s reputation for academic rigor and international curriculum is a powerful draw for families, professionals, and corporate executives seeking a world-class education for their children. The school’s IB program, known for emphasizing critical thinking, global awareness, and cultural engagement, attracts residents from across the country and beyond.

    For the Town, this means a steady influx of families with a keen interest in education and a commitment to community involvement. Many families move to Westlake specifically because of the Academy, elevating property values and reinforcing Westlake’s reputation as a highly desirable place to live. “Many of my clients are moving from places like California and other states specifically for Westlake Academy. These families quickly become active in the community, participating in and supporting Westlake Academy events, which enriches the community for everyone”, says realtor and Westlake resident, Susan Mathews.

    Realtors like Mathews consistently highlight Westlake Academy as a key selling point when marketing homes, and for good reason.  Families are willing to invest in properties that give their children access to top-tier education. “The presence of WA consistently drives property values and speeds up sales, with homes here often seeing a premium per square foot compared to neighboring areas. The demand created by the Academy’s unique academic standing means properties generally spend less time on the market and attract competitive offers, underscoring the financial benefits of buying in Westlake”, says Mathews.

    While Westlake maintains a low tax rate, Westlake Academy provides an exceptional return on investment for the Town and its residents. Because it operates as a public charter school, the Academy is funded through a combination of state funding, local contributions, and charitable donations which creates a funding mechanism that shares the cost of education across a diverse and more stable revenue source.

    The symbiotic relationship between the Academy and the Town is also evident in Westlake’s appeal to businesses. Many corporate executives, drawn to Westlake’s schools and lifestyle, have established global or national headquarters in the area. These companies benefit from the highly educated workforce and strong community values that Westlake Academy helps to cultivate. In turn, this bolsters the Town’s economic development and ensures long-term financial sustainability.

    By attracting families and businesses alike, it helps secure Westlake’s position as a premier destination in North Texas. The Town, in turn, supports the Academy by maintaining high standards for residential and commercial developments, fostering a community that values education, and providing the resources necessary for the school to thrive.

    Westlake Academy’s presence is integral to the Town’s identity and success. In return, the Town’s commitment to fostering a supportive environment ensures that the Academy will continue to shine as a key asset, elevating Westlake’s reputation as a premier destination for living, learning, and growth.  A stronger Academy makes for a stronger Town.  An important partnership that brings Westlake Together.

  • Westlake Budget Information

    Westlake Town Council Approves Budget for Fiscal Year 2024-2025

    In a unanimous decision during the September 16 meeting, the Westlake Town Council approved the budget for the 2024-2025 fiscal year. The budget projects $28 million in total revenues and $24.5 million in expenditures, alongside $5.1 million in inter-fund transfers. The budget also allocates $4.6 million in total contributions to the Town’s fund balance and retained earnings, which includes $1.5 million earmarked in the general fund for future capital spending.

    For the fourth consecutive year, Westlake's property tax rate remains flat at $0.16788 per $100 of taxable value. While this tax rate remains the lowest in North Texas and the bottom third across the state, the average Westlake homeowner can expect to pay about $304 more yearly in property taxes than last year due to increased property values. However, less than 10% of a property owner’s total tax bill actually goes directly to the Town.

    Key highlights from the approved budget include:

    - A $1.7 million subsidy to Westlake Academy, ensuring continued support for the Town’s educational offerings.

    - Funding for 5.25 full-time employees, including three new firefighters and two public works staff.

    - Adjustments to staff compensation to remain competitive in the job market.

    - A forecasted decrease in revenues of approximately $153,000, largely due to a full reduction of $4 million in revenues that was not realized due to an increase in sales tax and interest earnings.

    The budget approval marks the culmination of months of collaboration between the Town Council and staff, whose goal was to maintain a conservative financial approach while delivering high-quality services to Westlake residents. Despite rising property values, the low tax rate reflects the Town’s dedication to fiscal responsibility while meeting community needs.

    "Our commitment is to ensure Westlake continues to offer exceptional services while maintaining one of the lowest tax rates in the state," says Mayor Kim Greaves. "The hard work behind this budget reflects our promise to save residents money and provide the level of service they deserve."

    As Westlake looks ahead, the approved budget will help sustain the Town’s growth while supporting future infrastructure and capital projects. This balance between conservative budgeting and strategic investments ensures that Westlake remains a premier community with an emphasis on transparency and fiscal responsibility for years to come.

  • Late Fees Applied to Unpaid Water Bills

    Beginning July 1, 2024, the Town of Westlake will start charging fees for those who are late paying their utility bills.  This was prompted by your Town Council passing an ordinance during our June 3, 2024 meeting.  The ordinance was passed due to a growing balance of unpaid bills Town-wide.  In fact, 21% of the average monthly outstanding revenue is delinquent on any given month. That equates to more than $141,000 in unpaid bills last year alone.

    Council and Town staff felt it was essential to start charging the late fees due to aging infrastructure that desperately needs those funds.  If the bills aren’t paid, that would mean rates would have to be raised for all customers, including those who do submit their payments on time.  That’s a scenario everyone wants to avoid.

    Town staff identified three categories of water customers and how the late fees would apply to each.  They include residential, commercial, and temporary.  Temporary refers to builders and developers.  A breakdown of the costs for each category can be found here.  Failure to pay the bills and late fees would result in water service being cut off.  The next step would be to turn the matter over to a collection agency.  It's important to note that the late fees WILL NOT be retroactive for past unpaid bills, only those starting on July 1, 2024.

    One of the significant reasons for non-payment is that many customers’ credit cards expire and aren’t updated.  So when it comes time to auto draft from the card, it’s no longer valid.  That’s why staff recommends you set up your account to autdraft from your banking account instead of your credit card. That will eliminate the need to update your account info when your credit card expires.  And there’s no fee to sign up through your bank account. You can find the bank auto draft information on our website.

    We understand you may have a lot of questions. That’s why we’ve uploaded the entire presentation to Council to our website. You can find a complete breakdown of how much the late fees are, when they’re assessed, as well as steps to resolve the issue. If that doesn’t answer all your questions, you can email our Utility and Facility Coordinator, Dianna Orender.

    We appreciate your help and believe this is the best way to solve an issue that could result in costly rate hikes in the future.  Thank you!

  • Crime Stopping Cameras Installed

    The Town of Westlake is close to completing a project to help keep our residents safer than ever.  On February 5th, Council approved $116,000 to install cameras at all Town entrances and Westlake Academy.  The license plate reading camera system will reduce and deter criminal activity in Westlake.  The cameras register the make, model, and color of the vehicle and the license plate number and compare that information to secure federal and state data banks which hold information on stolen vehicles, active arrest warrants, and Amber and Silver Alerts.

    If the information matches the information in the database, the Keller Police Department is alerted within 30 seconds of the vehicle passing by the camera.  Keller PD can then locate the vehicle and investigate the situation.  Similar cameras are installed in Trophy Club and have already helped reduce crime there. 30 cameras will soon be installed around Westlake.  

    It’s important to note that the cameras will only capture video of vehicles entering Town limits, not leaving.  The images will be held for 30 days then deleted.  Police will only monitor vehicles attached to felonies and not misdemeanors.  Also, this is not a facial recognition tool; it only takes video of the back of the vehicle where the license plate can be seen.  The driver/passenger’s faces are never seen. It’s also not used for traffic enforcement either, so you won’t get any tickets in the mail because of the cameras.  And most importantly, your personal information is safe. 

    Keller Police will provide the Town with monthly reports showing how many vehicles were tied to felonies/outstanding warrants and how many arrests were made.  A map of where the cameras will be installed around Town is below. For more information about the cameras, please click on this link.

  • Westlake's Tax Rate Approved

    During the September 25th meeting, Town Council continued our legacy of having one of the lowest tax rates in the entire state by approving the maximum rate of 0.16788 per $100 assessed valuation.  The approved rate did not increase and remained the same as last year.

    The Town of Westlake takes our role as stewards of taxpayer resources very seriously.  Every person involved, from the Town Council members to the municipal staff team, does their best to keep taxes low while still providing you with the high level of service you expect.  The rate remains low to mirror our conservative and long-term planning budget practices. That’s why a low-level tax rate was established and maintained.

    Check out the graphic below. Westlake’s tax rate continues to be well below many of our neighboring cities and peer communities.

    So where is your money going?  For the typical Westlake household, just 9.56% of every dollar goes to the Town while 11.07% of your property taxes go to the county, 17.46% goes to special districts like the hospital district and Tarrant County Community College, and 61.91% goes to the school districts of Carroll, Northwest, or Keller ISDs depending upon the boundary in which your residence falls within.  The graphic below helps illustrate this further. 

    Amazingly, from those 17 cents, the core Town services such as quality public safety, planning and development, public works, the finance department, and Westlake Academy are funded. So how is that possible? To stretch that money, the Town utilizes a “shared services” model where several staff members step outside their traditional roles associated with each department within the municipality and work in a cross-functional manner between the Town and Westlake Academy. This helps us keep our overhead to a minimum while providing exceptional service. 

    In addition to receiving money through property taxes, the Town of Westlake receives revenue from sales taxes, building permits, and other fees to fund our daily operations. This work includes preserving a high quality of living for our residents, maintaining stellar roads and trails, and providing top-notch emergency services, which makes Westlake such a unique and idyllic place to call home. We truly are “Distinctive by Design”.
